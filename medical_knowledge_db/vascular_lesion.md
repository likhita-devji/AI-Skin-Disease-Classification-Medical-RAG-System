# Clinical Reference Guide: Vascular Lesions (VASC)

## Overview
Vascular skin lesions arise from abnormal blood vessels (capillaries, veins, arteries, or lymphatic channels) in or underneath the skin. This category includes **Cherry Angiomas**, **Angiokeratomas**, **Pyogenic Granulomas**, **Port-Wine Stains**, and **Hemangiomas**.

## Key Clinical Subtypes
1. **Cherry Angioma (De Morgan Spots)**:
   - Extremely common benign capillary proliferations in adults >30 years.
   - Bright red, purple, or violaceous small papules or macules.
   - Completely harmless and non-malignant.
2. **Pyogenic Granuloma (Lobular Capillary Hemangioma)**:
   - Rapidly growing, friable red vascular nodule.
   - Bleeds profusely with minor trauma. Frequently occurs during pregnancy or at sites of insect bites/wounds.
3. **Angiokeratoma**:
   - Dark red, blue, or black hyperkeratotic papules with a rough surface.
   - Can clinically mimic dark nodular melanoma or thrombosed vascular lesions.
4. **Infantile Hemangioma**:
   - Benign vascular tumor of infancy ("strawberry hemangioma"). Grows rapidly during early months before undergoing spontaneous involution over childhood years.

## Diagnostic Signs
- **Diascopy (Blanching Test)**: Applying pressure with a glass slide causes most benign vascular lesions to blanch (fade) as blood is pressed out of capillaries.

## Management & Therapies
- Benign vascular growths require no medical intervention.
- Pyogenic granulomas are treated with curettage and electrodesiccation or surgical excision.
- Cosmetically undesired cherry angiomas or port-wine stains respond exceptionally well to Pulsed Dye Laser (PDL) or Intense Pulsed Light (IPL).
