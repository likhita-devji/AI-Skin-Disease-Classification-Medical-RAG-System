# Clinical Reference Guide: Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)

## Overview
Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) is the single most common malignant skin cancer in humans worldwide. It arises from the basal layer of the epidermis and hair follicle structures. BCC expands slowly and rarely metastasizes to distant organs (<0.1%), but it can cause substantial local tissue destruction and disfigurement if neglected.

## Clinical Presentations & Features
1. **Nodular BCC (Most Common ~60%)**: Pearly or translucent papule/nodule with rolled borders, arborizing telangiectasias (visible blood vessels), and a central ulceration or dip ("rodent ulcer").
2. **Superficial BCC**: Erythematous, scaly patch resembling eczema or psoriasis, often with a thread-like pearly border. Frequently found on the trunk and limbs.
3. **Infiltrative / Morpheaform BCC**: Firm, scar-like whitish or yellowish plaque with ill-defined borders. Higher recurrence rate.
4. **Pigmented BCC**: Contains brown, black, or blue melanin pigment; can clinically mimic melanoma.

## Risk Factors
- Chronic UV exposure (cumulative lifetime sun exposure).
- Intermittent intense sun exposure and history of sunburns.
- Advanced age (>50 years old).
- Immunosuppression (organ transplant recipients).
- Exposure to ionizing radiation or inorganic arsenic.
- Genetic syndromes: Gorlin-Goltz syndrome (Nevoid Basal Cell Carcinoma Syndrome).

## Diagnosis & Dermoscopy
- **Dermoscopic Signs**: Absence of pigment network, leaf-like areas, spoke-wheel structures, blue-grey globules, and branch-like telangiectatic vessels.
- **Biopsy**: Punch or shave biopsy confirms histopathological subtype.

## Treatment Options
- **Mohs Micrographic Surgery (MMS)**: Highest cure rate (>99%). Indicated for facial BCCs, recurrent tumors, high-risk subtypes, or ill-defined margins.
- **Standard Surgical Excision**: 4mm clinical surgical margins for low-risk non-facial lesions.
- **Curettage & Electrodesiccation (ED&C)**: Suitable for small, superficial non-facial lesions.
- **Topical Therapies**: Imiquimod 5% cream or 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) for superficial BCC when surgery is contraindicated.
- **Hedgehog Pathway Inhibitors (Vismodegib, Sonidegib)**: Systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic BCC.

## Long-Term Management
- Regular 6-to-12-month skin surveillance, as patients with one BCC have a ~30-50% risk of developing another primary BCC within 5 years.
