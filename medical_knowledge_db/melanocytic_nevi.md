# Clinical Reference Guide: Melanocytic Nevi (NV) - Common Moles

## Overview
Melanocytic Nevi (commonly referred to as moles) are benign neoplasms or hamartomas composed of nests of melanocytes (nevus cells). Almost all individuals have at least a few nevi, with the average adult possessing between 10 and 40 nevi.

## Classification of Melanocytic Nevi
1. **Junctional Nevi**: Nevus cells located strictly at the dermal-epidermal junction. Typically flat, brown-to-black macules.
2. **Compound Nevi**: Nevus cells present both at the dermo-epidermal junction and within the dermis. Slightly raised, light-to-dark brown papules.
3. **Intradermal Nevi**: Nevus cells located entirely within the dermis. Elevated, dome-shaped, skin-colored, or light brown papules, often appearing on the face/neck in adults.
4. **Dysplastic / Atypical Nevi**: Moles with irregular features (border irregularity, color variation, size >5mm). Individuals with multiple dysplastic nevi have an elevated lifetime risk of melanoma.

## Monitoring & Self-Examination Guidelines
- **The "Ugly Duckling" Sign**: A mole that looks noticeably different from all other moles on an individual's body warrants clinical investigation.
- **ABCDE Criteria Tracking**: Monitor for changes in Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter, or Evolution.

## Management Protocol
- Normal melanocytic nevi require no treatment.
- Biopsy (excisional or deep shave) is mandatory if a mole displays rapid growth, asymmetry, spontaneous bleeding, or new symptoms.
