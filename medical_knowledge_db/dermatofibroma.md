# Clinical Reference Guide: Dermatofibroma (DF)

## Overview
Dermatofibroma (Benign Fibrous Histiocytoma) is a common, non-cancerous dermal skin papule or nodule. It is composed of proliferated dermal fibroblasts, histiocytes, and collagen fibers. Dermatofibromas most commonly occur in young to middle-aged adults, predominantly women, frequently on the lower legs.

## Etiology & Pathogenesis
The exact cause remains unclear, but dermatofibromas are widely regarded as a reactive proliferation triggered by minor trauma, such as insect bites, minor punctures, or ingrown hairs.

## Clinical Features
- **Firmness**: Exceptionally hard or button-like on palpation.
- **The "Fitzpatrick Pinch Sign"**: When gently pinched from opposite sides, the central portion dimples inward into the skin, rather than elevating.
- **Color**: Reddish-brown, dull yellow, pink, or brownish-violet.
- **Size**: Typically small (3mm to 10mm).
- **Symptoms**: Usually asymptomatic, though occasionally itchy or mildly tender when bumped.

## Dermoscopic Features
- **Central White Patch**: A central white scar-like area surrounded by a delicate pigment network at the periphery.

## Management & Prognosis
- Completely benign with no risk of malignant transformation.
- No medical treatment required unless symptomatic, frequently traumatized (e.g., during shaving), or cosmetically inconvenient.
- Surgical removal requires full-thickness dermal excision because the tumor extends deep into subcutaneous fat; simple shave biopsy often results in recurrence.
