# Clinical Reference Guide: Melanoma (MEL)

## Overview
Melanoma is the most aggressive and dangerous form of skin cancer, originating in melanocytes—the pigment-producing cells of the epidermis. While accounting for a small percentage of skin cancer cases, melanoma is responsible for the majority of skin cancer-related fatalities if not diagnosed at an early stage.

## Clinical Features & ABCDE Diagnostic Criteria
Dermatologists rely on the standard **ABCDE** clinical rules for identifying suspected melanoma lesions:
- **A - Asymmetry**: One half of the spot does not match the other half.
- **B - Border**: The edges are irregular, ragged, notched, or blurred.
- **C - Color**: The color is non-uniform and may include varying shades of brown, black, pink, red, white, or blue.
- **D - Diameter**: The spot is typically larger than 6mm (about the size of a pencil eraser), though melanomas can sometimes be smaller.
- **E - Evolving**: The lesion changes in size, shape, color, or symptoms (itching, bleeding, crusting) over time.

## Subtypes of Melanoma
1. **Superficial Spreading Melanoma**: Accounts for ~70% of cases. Grows radially across the outer skin layer before penetrating deeper.
2. **Nodular Melanoma**: Highly aggressive; grows vertically downward into deeper skin layers quickly. Appears as a dark, dome-shaped bump.
3. **Lentigo Maligna Melanoma**: Common in older individuals on chronically sun-exposed skin (face, neck, arms).
4. **Acral Lentiginous Melanoma**: Occurs on palms, soles of feet, or under fingernails/toenails. Unrelated to sun exposure.

## Risk Factors
- Excessive exposure to Ultraviolet (UV) radiation (sunlight, tanning beds).
- History of severe blistering sunburns during childhood or adolescence.
- High mole count (>50 common moles or atypical/dysplastic nevi).
- Fair skin, light hair, blue/green eyes, or tendency to freckle.
- Family history of melanoma or genetic mutations (e.g., CDKN2A, BRAF).

## Diagnostic Workup & Staging
- **Dermoscopy**: High-magnification polarized light exam to assess pigment network structure.
- **Excisional Biopsy**: Gold standard for definitive diagnosis. Provides Breslow depth thickness measurement.
- **AJCC Staging System**: Stages I-IV based on tumor thickness (Breslow depth), ulceration status, regional lymph node involvement, and distant metastasis.

## Treatment Protocols
- **Stage I & II (Localized)**: Surgical wide local excision with clear margins. Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) if Breslow depth >0.8mm.
- **Stage III (Regional Lymph Node Metastasis)**: Lymph node dissection, adjuvant immunotherapy (Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab), or targeted therapy (Dabrafenib + Trametinib for BRAF V600 mutations).
- **Stage IV (Metastatic)**: Dual checkpoint blockade (Ipilimumab + Nivolumab), targeted therapy, or clinical trials.

## Patient Precautions & Prevention
- **Strict Sun Safety**: Broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen, protective clothing, and UV-blocking sunglasses.
- **Regular Self-Examinations**: Monthly total-body skin exams to detect evolving moles.
- **Annual Dermatological Audits**: Professional skin checks for high-risk individuals.
