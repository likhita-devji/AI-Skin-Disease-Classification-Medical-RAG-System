# Clinical Reference Guide: Actinic Keratosis & Intraepithelial Carcinoma (AKIEC)

## Overview
Actinic Keratosis (AK), also referred to as solar keratosis, is a pre-cancerous epidermoid skin lesion caused by chronic ultraviolet radiation damage. It represents dysplasia of epidermal keratinocytes. If left untreated, AK can progress to Invasive Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) in approximately 5-10% of cases over time. Intraepithelial Carcinoma (Bowen's Disease) represents full-thickness squamous carcinoma in situ.

## Clinical Appearance
- Rough, dry, scaly, sand-paper-like feeling on palpation (often felt before distinctly seen).
- Color ranges from skin-toned, pink, red, to yellowish-brown.
- Typically ranges from 2mm to 6mm in size.
- Located on sun-exposed anatomical sites: forehead, scalp (in balding men), ears, cheeks, nose, backs of hands, and forearms.

## Dermoscopic Features
- **Red Starburst / Strawberry Pattern**: Erythematous background with prominent follicular openings surrounded by white halos.
- **Keratin Scales**: Yellowish-white surface scale.

## Risk Factors
- Cumulative lifetime sun exposure and tanning bed use.
- Fair skin types (Fitzpatrick types I and II).
- Immunosuppression (organ transplant recipients have a 250-fold increased risk of SCC/AK).

## Management & Clinical Interventions
1. **Lesion-Directed Therapy**:
   - **Cryotherapy (Liquid Nitrogen)**: First-line treatment for isolated AK lesions. Freezes dysplastic keratinocytes.
   - **Curettage or Shave Excision**: Used for thicker, hypertrophic lesions to rule out underlying invasive SCC.
2. **Field-Directed Therapy** (For widespread AK across a regional area):
   - **5-Fluorouracil (5-FU 5%) Cream**: Topical chemotherapy applied for 2 to 4 weeks.
   - **Imiquimod 3.75% / 5% Cream**: Immune response modifier that stimulates local interferon response.
   - **Photodynamic Therapy (PDT)**: Topical photosensitizer (ALA/MAL) followed by light activation.

## Prevention Guidelines
- Daily application of broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher).
- Wearing wide-brimmed hats and UV-protective clothing outdoors.
- Early clinical intervention for any new or scaly non-healing skin patches.
